Bro, your friend is not completely wrong, but I think he is pointing at the wrong problem.

Your website is already much better technically than a lot of small handmade-business websites. The issue is more:

“Why would I remember Ashma Creations instead of the next handmade-gift website?”

That's a brand/product-experience problem, not an SEO problem.

And honestly, you have a huge opportunity here because you're a small handmade business. You can do things that big gift websites can't.

The biggest mistake: trying to make the website “fancier”

I wouldn't add 50 animations, 3D effects, crazy gradients, etc.

That can actually make it look more like a generic AI-generated ecommerce site.

Instead, I'd make Ashma Creations feel like a real handmade studio.

Think:

Not:

“An online store selling handmade gifts.”

But:

“A little handmade studio where every gift is made personally for someone.”

That difference should appear throughout the website.

1. Make the homepage tell the Ashma story

Instead of immediately:

Handmade Gifts
Browse our products...

Have a section like:

Made by hand. Made for someone special. ❤️

Every Ashma Creations piece is handmade by us, one creation at a time.
Choose a design, customize it for someone you love, and we'll turn your idea into a keepsake they'll remember.

Then:

You choose → We customize → We handcraft → You gift

That's much more memorable.

2. Show the HUMAN behind the products

This is probably the biggest thing I'd add.

Your friend sees a website.

You want the visitor to feel:

“Oh, there are actual people making these.”

Create a beautiful section:

Meet the Maker

Photo of your wife making a pipe-cleaner flower.

Something like:

Hi, I'm Ashima 👋

Ashma Creations started with a simple love for handmade things.

What began as making little flowers became something much more meaningful — creating personalized gifts for people celebrating birthdays, anniversaries, festivals and special moments.

Every order is made by hand, packed by us and sent with love.

That immediately differentiates you from generic gift stores.

3. Show the actual making process

This is perfect for your business.

Have:

From Pipe Cleaner → Keepsake 🌸

01 — Choose your creation
Browse our handmade designs.

02 — Make it yours
Choose colours, names, messages or other customizations.

03 — We handcraft it
Each piece is carefully made by hand.

04 — Packed with love
Your finished creation is safely prepared for its journey.

And use real photos, not AI.

You already have that excellent banner statement:

OUR PRODUCT PHOTOS ARE 100% REAL

Lean into that.

4. Add “Customize This” as a core experience

You said:

Every item is customisable.

That's potentially your USP.

Don't hide it in a sentence.

Make customization one of the main things people experience.

For example on every product:

💬 Discuss customization on WhatsApp

Add to cart

especially while you don't have a payment/checkout system yet.

5. Create a “Made for you” section

This could become an Ashma signature.

For example:

Don't see exactly what you're imagining?

“I want a pink flower pot for my sister with her name on it.”

“I want a small bouquet under ₹500 for my girlfriend.”

“Can you make something for my parents' anniversary?”

We can try. 🌸

[Tell us your idea →]

This is very powerful for a handmade business.

You aren't competing with Amazon.

You're saying:

Bring us your idea.

Eventually this becomes one of the strongest parts of the site.

That kind of content makes handmade products feel valuable.

11. Don't make everything perfectly polished

This sounds weird, but it's important.

Big ecommerce stores are polished.

Handmade brands should have personality.

A small handwritten note saying:

“Made especially for you 💕”

can be more memorable than another fancy animation.

You could even include photos of handwritten thank-you cards.

Right now you have:

shadows

All nice.

But many modern small-business websites have exactly this.

I'd develop 2–3 Ashma signatures.

For example:

Handwritten-style little notes:

A distinctive “Ashma” pink/paper aesthetic.

Not 20 different decorative elements.

Just a few things that people associate with Ashma.

And here's the BIG one
Your website should not try to be the thing that makes Ashma popular.

Your products + content + story should make Ashma popular.

The website should be the place where people arrive and think:

“Oh wow, this is a real handmade brand.”

That's why your Reddit idea is actually interesting.

Imagine someone sees your Reddit post:

“I spent 3 hours making this tiny flower pot for my sister. Would you give this as a gift?”

They click your profile.

Then your website has:

Made by hand. Made for someone special.

Then they see:

real making photos
customization
your wife's story
actual products
customer photos

Now there's a story connecting everything.

If this were MY website, I'd prioritize these 5 things

Don't rebuild everything.

🔥 Priority 1 — Meet the Maker

Real photo + Ashima's story.

🔥 Priority 2 — Customization experience

Make “Customize it” a major feature.

🔥 Priority 3 — Behind the Craft

Real photos/videos showing how products are made.

🔥 Priority 4 — “Have an idea? Tell us.”

Let people request completely custom creations.

🔥 Priority 5 — Gift Finder

Recipient + occasion + budget → recommendations.

Those five would make a much bigger difference than adding more SEO markup or another fancy animation.

And honestly, #4 could become your strongest differentiator:

You imagine it. We make it. 🌸

Tell Ashma what you're looking for →

That is something a generic gift store can't convincingly say.

If you want Ashma to become a recognizable brand rather than just another handmade-products website, I'd build the entire brand around personalization + the human maker story + real handmade craftsmanship.
